# Market Pulse — Week of November 25, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date tech layoffs have now exceeded **240,000 workers** across nearly 600 tracked events — a sharp escalation from the ~95,000 mark in mid-April. November alone has logged **54 announcements and over 5,300 confirmed cuts** so far.

- **Canva** executed its first-ever mass restructuring, affecting **1,271 employees (22% of its workforce)**. CEO Melanie Perkins framed it as a reorganization for the AI era after growth forecasts were cut from 30% to 20%. Affected teams span product, growth, and platform. Severance packages reportedly include six months' salary.
- **Meta** filed WARN notices in New York covering **932 workers** in early November — a continuation of ongoing "org simplification" ahead of heavier AI agent deployment.
- **Block** initiated a second round of cuts after eliminating more than 4,000 roles earlier this year. **Squarespace** is cutting roughly 300.
- **Intel** began another layoff round targeting its IT organization. **Qualcomm** is reportedly cutting up to 1,000 positions in India.
- **Disney** is planning thousands of cuts aimed at reducing annual costs by 20% — the entertainment sector's displacement wave continues to deepen.
- **Microsoft's October voluntary exit offer**, covering roughly 60,000 eligible employees, is not counted in the trackers but represents significant soft attrition in progress.

**What's different this month:** the Canva cut is a bellwether. A design-tool company that employs many of the creative professionals AI is meant to augment is itself shedding 22% of headcount because AI changed its business model. When the tools-for-creatives company reorganizes around AI, the signal is unmistakable: even the adjacent ecosystem — not just the end users — is being reshaped.

## Emerging Roles

The role landscape is splitting into two lanes: narrow technical AI positions and a new management layer for human-AI coordination.

- **AI Agent Operator / Supervisor** — 44% of enterprises surveyed by Box say they are actively hiring people to configure, monitor, and troubleshoot AI agents in production. Mercer is coining titles like "Agent Supervisor" and "Customer Success Orchestration Manager" — operational roles that bridge technology and workflow management.
- **AI Learning & Development Lead** — 30% of companies surveyed are considering hiring someone to manage internal AI upskilling. This role sits between HR, training, and technology — it's the person who decides what the workforce needs to learn next.
- **AI Process Redesign Lead** — 25% of respondents are exploring a role dedicated to rearchitecting workflows around human-agent collaboration. Not a one-time consultant engagement — an ongoing function.
- **AI Sales Specialist** — sales accounts for 17% of open AI-specific roles, making it the second-largest category after engineering. These aren't traditional sales reps — they need enough technical fluency to demo agent products and scope enterprise integrations.
- **Trades and Physical Roles** — career advisers are increasingly steering displaced white-collar workers toward nursing, electrical work, plumbing, and HVAC. These aren't consolation prizes — they're stable demand with clear licensing paths and near-zero AI exposure.

**New this month:** the "Agent Supervisor" cluster is the signal to watch. When enterprises start hiring dedicated humans to oversee fleets of AI agents, the management layer of the AI-era org chart is crystallizing. The ratio being floated — one human per 50–100 routine agents — implies a substantial new employment category if agent deployment scales as projected.

## Skills in Demand

The skills market is becoming more legible. AI skills carry a measurable premium, but human skills haven't been displaced — they've become the differentiator.

- **AI skills are the fastest-growing requirement.** SHRM's Q3 2026 review reports AI and machine learning mentions in job postings more than doubled year-over-year (+119%). The Bipartisan Policy Center's Lightcast data shows AI skill mentions up 165% over the same period.
- **But communication is still #1.** Qarera's analysis of 360,000+ postings puts communication at 23.1% of all skills requested — ahead of AI at 19.8%. CodeSignal's 50-million-posting analysis confirms interpersonal skills appear in 16% of listings. Employers want people who can work with AI *and* explain what it's doing.
- **The AI wage premium is real.** PwC's 2026 AI Jobs Barometer reports that jobs requiring AI skills grew 69% — roughly 8× faster than the broader market. ZipRecruiter's survey confirms employers are willing to pay more for demonstrated AI competency.
- **Skills-based hiring continues to gain ground.** NACE's 2026 survey found 70% of employers now use skills-based hiring, up from 65% last year. Portfolios and demonstrated work are outpacing credentials.
- **Entry-level AI roles remain scarce.** Only 3% of tracked AI job openings are entry-level or internship positions. PwC notes that AI-exposed entry-level roles are increasingly requiring senior-level skills — a troubling bottleneck for career changers trying to break in.
- **Overall hiring is soft.** Hacker News "Who is Hiring?" postings are down 37% year-over-year. The U.S. economy has an estimated 2–3 million fewer white-collar jobs than pre-pandemic growth trends would predict. SHRM pegs national labor demand at 97.7% of pre-pandemic levels — close, but still below the waterline.

**The entry-level squeeze is the structural problem.** AI is creating well-paying roles but gating them behind experience requirements that lock out the people who need them most. The 3% entry-level figure means traditional ladders are broken — alternative on-ramps (apprenticeships, project portfolios, skills-based assessments) matter more than ever.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **DOL proposes sweeping PERM overhaul (November 16–17):** The Department of Labor published its first comprehensive reform of the green-card labor certification process in nearly 20 years. Key changes: the layoff look-back period would extend from 6 months to 2 years, recruitment windows would lengthen, and DOL is considering whether to exclude fully remote positions from PERM eligibility. The extended layoff look-back could incentivize employers to retrain existing workers rather than replace them with sponsored hires — a meaningful lever for workforce retention.
- **Independent contractor rule replaced (November 19):** DOL rescinded the 2024 independent contractor regulations and replaced them with a streamlined analysis resembling the 2021 framework. For displaced workers considering freelance or gig work, the classification landscape just shifted again — worth monitoring for anyone building a portfolio career.
- **Apprenticeship incentive payments now open:** Eligible registered apprenticeship sponsors can apply for up to **$6,000 per apprentice** in designated high-growth industries. This builds on the April apprenticeship initiative and puts real dollars behind skills-based pathways.
- **Workforce Pell is live:** Pell Grant eligibility now extends to students in qualifying short-term workforce training programs for the 2026–27 award year. This is the first time federal financial aid has been available for sub-degree training at this scale — a direct on-ramp for career changers who can't afford full-time enrollment.
- **ZipRecruiter optimism signal:** 30% of companies surveyed say AI will lead to *more* hiring in 2027, up from 19% the prior year. That's not a majority, but the direction of the trend suggests some employers are moving past the "cut costs with AI" phase into "scale with AI + humans."

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

**The displacement numbers are sobering — 240,000+ cuts in 2026 — but the infrastructure for rebuilding is more concrete than it's ever been.**

Canva's restructuring is the week's clearest signal: even companies that build creative tools are reorganizing around AI, not just the industries those tools serve. If you're in a role that touches content, design, marketing, or operations, the restructuring pressure is no longer hypothetical.

But the response architecture is maturing too. Workforce Pell means short-term training programs now have federal financial aid behind them. Apprenticeship incentives put up to $6,000 per person into employer-sponsored learning. And the skills market is rewarding demonstrated capability over credentials — 70% of employers now hire skills-first.

**If you're navigating a transition right now:** the entry-level AI bottleneck is real (only 3% of AI openings), so don't aim for "AI engineer" as your first move. Instead, look at the adjacent roles that are actually hiring — agent operations, AI-augmented sales, process redesign, learning & development. These roles need someone who understands both the technology and the humans using it. That's a career changer's advantage, not a disadvantage.

**If you're building skills:** communication remains the #1 requested skill in job postings, ahead of AI. The highest-value profile isn't "can use AI" or "can talk to humans" — it's both. Pick one AI tool, get competent with it, and pair that with the domain expertise you already have. That combination is what the 70% skills-based hiring market is looking for.

The white-collar job gap is real and widening. But the on-ramps — Pell-funded training, apprenticeship dollars, skills-based hiring — are finally catching up to the scale of the problem. Use them.
