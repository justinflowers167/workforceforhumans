# Market Pulse — Week of July 28, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date tech layoffs have crossed **205,000 workers** across **322 layoff events** — roughly **980 per day** since January 1. July alone has added 13,500+ cuts across 25 companies. WARN Act filings tell a wider story: **270,641 employees** affected across 44 states in 2026, well beyond tech.

- **Disney** cut several hundred positions on **July 21** in its third round of 2026 layoffs. **Pixar** (~116 cuts), **National Geographic**, **ESPN**, and **ABC News** were hardest hit. The cuts are structural — CEO Josh D'Amaro's "One Disney" reorganization is collapsing overlapping divisions, not responding to a revenue shortfall.
- **Monday.com** eliminated **630 employees (~20% of workforce)** on **July 22**, explicitly pivoting toward an "AI Work Platform." ~350 of the cuts were in Israel. The company still projects 20% revenue growth — this is investment rebalancing, not contraction.
- **Patreon** laid off **93 employees (20%)** on **July 23**. CEO Jack Conte notably said the cuts are *not* AI-driven — citing "changing market conditions." Sixteen weeks severance. Creator-economy companies continue to struggle with sustainable business models.
- **Amazon's AGI unit** made undisclosed cuts in late July, redirecting resources from research toward customer-facing AI products. An additional ~80 cuts hit the pharmacy org. Amazon has now cut 57,000+ corporate jobs since 2022.
- **Mobileye/Moovit** cut **30% of Moovit's workforce** (~60 employees) on **July 24**, pivoting the ride-planning app from B2B transit toward its parent's robotaxi strategy. A case study in how AI reshapes jobs even at companies *building* AI — the transit-planning team is being replaced by robotaxi-operations roles.
- **New Jersey** filed WARN notices for **2,400+ cuts in July alone**, bringing the state's 2026 total past 10,800. Samsung (739, relocating HQ to Texas), Novartis (322), Verizon (282), and Mars Wrigley all contributed.

**What's different this week:** Monday.com's move is the clearest example yet of a profitable company cutting a fifth of its workforce not because it's struggling, but because it's pivoting its entire product around AI. That framing — "we're growing, and we're cutting" — is becoming the norm, not the exception. Oracle (21,000 in June), PayPal (4,760 in May), and Microsoft (4,800 earlier in July) all made similar moves from positions of financial strength. When cuts are driven by strategy rather than survival, the displacement is harder to see coming and harder to wait out.

## Emerging Roles

The AI role landscape is consolidating. "Prompt engineer" as a standalone title is fading — LinkedIn data shows postings with that *title* declining even as postings listing it as a *skill* grew ~250%. The expertise didn't vanish; it folded into broader roles. Several specialized positions are crystallizing:

- **AI Agent Architect** — designs the cognitive architecture of autonomous agent systems. With ~60% of new enterprise software projects now including an agentic component, this is no longer experimental hiring. Comp ranges $150K–$300K+.
- **AgentOps Engineer** — the operational counterpart to the architect. Monitors, debugs, and maintains multi-agent systems in production. Think "DevOps for agents." Growing out of SRE and platform engineering teams.
- **Synthetic Data Curator** — creates artificial training datasets targeted at specific model weaknesses. A bridge role between domain expertise and ML engineering, accessible to non-CS backgrounds with strong analytical skills.
- **AI Systems Auditor** — evaluates deployed AI for bias, drift, and compliance. Demand accelerating as the PREPARE Act's WARN disclosure requirements and enterprise governance mandates take hold.
- **Human-AI Interaction Designer** — a UX specialization focused on agent interfaces, handoff flows, and trust calibration. Emerging in HR-tech, healthcare, and customer-facing product teams.
- **Clinical AI Validator / Medical AI Trainer** — healthcare-specific roles for professionals who validate AI outputs against clinical standards. Requires domain expertise first, AI fluency second.

**New this week:** the Moovit-to-robotaxi pivot illustrates a pattern worth watching. The same company that cut transit-planning roles is now hiring for robotaxi operations, fleet AI, and autonomous-systems integration. Displacement and emergence are happening *inside the same org*, sometimes in the same quarter. The new roles don't replace the old ones 1:1, but they're real — and they favor people who can bridge operational domain knowledge with AI systems thinking.

## Skills in Demand

The skills market is bifurcating sharply: AI fluency is table stakes for a growing share of roles, while the premium increasingly goes to people who can *operationalize* AI rather than just understand it.

- **AI/ML competencies** now appear in **89% of technical job postings**, with salaries $30K–$50K higher than equivalent non-AI roles. Workers with advanced AI skills earn **56% more** than peers in the same positions without them.
- **Agentic frameworks and orchestration** — hands-on experience building and managing autonomous AI workflows is the fastest-growing technical skill cluster. LangChain, vector databases, and multi-agent coordination are baseline expectations for senior roles.
- **AI video generation/editing** is the fastest-growing freelance skill on Upwork at **+329% YoY** — a signal that AI content creation is shifting from experimental to production.
- **Cybersecurity** appears in **65% of technical job postings** as a core requirement. The global shortfall stands at 3.4 million unfilled positions, 750,000 in the U.S. alone. AI is widening the attack surface faster than defenders can scale.
- **Operational AI integration** is overtaking research-oriented AI. Employer demand is shifting from "build the model" to "make the model work inside our business." MLOps has shown **9.8x growth** over five years.
- **Soft skills are rising, not falling.** AI-exposed junior roles are **7x more likely** to demand traditionally senior skills — leadership, strategic thinking, cross-functional communication. The floor for entry-level work is rising.

**The gap is enormous.** IDC projects a **$5.5 trillion** cost from AI skills shortages by year-end, with 90%+ of global enterprises facing critical gaps. Only one-third of employees report receiving any AI training in the past year. Freelancers who actively use AI tools earn **34% more per hour** than those who don't.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **Workforce Pell Grants went live July 1** (legal effective date July 20). Short-term Pell eligibility now covers **8–15 week programs** leading to in-demand credentials in IT, healthcare, skilled trades, vehicle operation, and early childhood education. Career changers with a bachelor's degree (but no graduate degree) are eligible. States are still standing up approval processes, so availability varies — but the mechanism is live and funded. This is the most significant expansion of federal training support in years.
- **DOGE officially ended July 4.** ~256,000 federal employees departed (11.3% of the workforce), 92.5% voluntarily. The claimed $215B in savings could not be verified by GAO. Federal agencies are now **actively re-hiring**: 104,000+ federal jobs posted in the first five months of 2026 (vs. ~68K in the last five months of 2025). HHS is targeting 12,000 hires; IRS is fast-tracking 8,000 after losing 25%+ of staff.
- **DOL's AI Workforce Hub** is nearing launch. The hub will centralize upskilling strategies, labor market data on AI's impact by industry, and training program directories. Authorized under the AI Workforce PREPARE Act, which also updates the WARN Act to require employers to disclose when AI is "a substantial factor" in mass layoffs.
- **WIOA Reauthorization** (A Stronger Workforce for America Act of 2026) advanced out of House committee in April but hasn't reached a floor vote. If enacted, it would auto-include Workforce Pell and Registered Apprenticeship on Eligible Training Provider Lists — streamlining access for displaced workers.
- **OPM Data Science Fellows Program** launched in spring 2026, targeting 250 cross-government AI/data hires. A small number, but a signal that the federal government is building in-house AI capacity rather than outsourcing it entirely.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

This week's clearest signal: **profitable companies are cutting strategically, not defensively — and federal re-hiring is creating a parallel on-ramp.**

Monday.com, Amazon, and Disney all cut from positions of strength. They're not shrinking — they're reshaping. That pattern makes traditional "wait for the recovery" logic less useful, because there may not be a downturn to recover *from*. The roles being cut aren't coming back in their current form. The roles being created — agent architects, AI auditors, synthetic data curators — require different skills, and they're real.

**If you're navigating a transition:** Workforce Pell Grants are the biggest new lever. An 8–15 week credentialed program, federally funded, available to career changers — that didn't exist three months ago. Pair that with the 104,000+ federal job postings now live (many explicitly seeking people who can integrate AI into agency operations), and there's a concrete path that doesn't require a two-year program or a bootcamp loan.

**If you're mid-career and employed:** the IDC $5.5 trillion skills-gap number matters to you, not just to policymakers. Your employer is statistically unlikely to be training you — only a third of workers got any AI training last year. The 56% wage premium for AI-skilled workers is real and growing. Freelancers using AI tools earn 34% more per hour. The return on self-directed upskilling has never been higher.

**If you're watching the federal space:** DOGE's end created a re-hiring wave. Agencies that lost a quarter of their staff are now posting thousands of positions. If you left federal service — or considered it but were deterred by the chaos — the window is open and the competition is thinner than usual.

The displacement is real and accelerating. But so are the on-ramps. The gap between them is closing faster in July 2026 than at any point this year.
