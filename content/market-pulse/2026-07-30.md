# Market Pulse — Week of July 30, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date layoffs have crossed the **205,000 mark** across **322 events** — roughly **980 workers per day** since January 1. Tech firms alone shed **139,156 jobs** in the first half of 2026, up **83% from H1 2025**. AI is now explicitly cited as a factor in **23% of all layoff announcements** this year.

- **Visa** cut **~2,600 jobs (7% of its workforce)** on July 28. CEO Ryan McInerney said AI has "reduced repetitive work and accelerated product development." The savings are being reinvested into stablecoin, cross-border payments, and B2B — not headcount. Technology and product teams took the heaviest hit.
- **Monday.com** eliminated **~620 employees (20%)** on July 22, restructuring around its "AI Work Platform" strategy. The company projects up to 20% revenue growth this year despite the cuts — another case of layoffs alongside strong financials.
- **Patreon** laid off **93 employees (20%)** on July 23. CEO Jack Conte cited "rapid market changes including AI," though the company clarified it isn't replacing workers with AI directly. Revenue was up 28% in 2025.
- **Intel** announced further cuts to its **Data Center and AI Group** on July 26, scale undisclosed pending Q2 earnings. This follows 40,000+ cuts in 2024–2025. The affected division posted $5.1B in Q1 revenue, up 22% YoY — cuts driven by structural realignment, not revenue decline.
- **Uber** cut community operations staff on July 22 (~10% of the function, 41 confirmed in California), its second restructuring in two months after trimming 23% of HR in June. Customer support is being reshaped around AI automation.
- **Verizon** is shedding **~3,000 roles** (500 corporate + 2,500 retail via the sale of 274 company-owned stores) effective mid-August.
- **K&L Gates**, a major international law firm, cut **~180 non-lawyer staff (10%)** after a multi-month operational review — a notable signal that professional services back-offices are now in the efficiency crosshairs.
- **Entain (Ladbrokes parent)** cut **500 jobs** in corporate, product, and technology roles — driven not by AI but by a UK gambling tax hike adding ~£200M in annual costs. A reminder that regulatory shocks still displace workers independently of the AI trend.

**What's different this week:** profitable companies are cutting at an accelerating pace. Visa, Monday.com, and Patreon all reported strong or growing revenue alongside their layoffs. The framing has shifted from "we're struggling" to "we're repositioning" — AI is the justification for doing more with fewer people even when the business is healthy. Two-thirds of CEOs surveyed say they plan to freeze or cut hiring through year-end.

## Emerging Roles

The agentic AI wave has moved from buzzword to hiring reality. Job postings mentioning agentic AI skills grew **280% year-over-year**, reaching roughly **90,000 US listings** with an average salary of **$190K** (senior roles at top companies: $300K–$550K).

- **Agentic AI Architect / Agent Supervisor** — designs, orchestrates, and oversees autonomous AI agent systems. The breakout role category of 2026, with companies like Deloitte, EY, Salesforce, Apple, and NVIDIA actively hiring. Required skills: access control, tool schemas, retrieval pipelines, memory design, error handling, and cost control.
- **AI Enablement Lead** — runs internal AI adoption programs, builds playbooks, coordinates with governance. An organizational role that didn't exist 12 months ago, now appearing across Fortune 500 companies transitioning from AI pilots to AI operations.
- **AI Sales Representative** — described by multiple hiring analysts as "arguably the hottest opportunity in 2026." Combines product knowledge with AI fluency to sell AI-powered solutions. The role bridges the gap between technical teams and buyer organizations.
- **Forward-Deployed AI Engineer** — embedded with business teams to customize AI systems for enterprise contexts. Listings surged **800%** in 2025; Anthropic is scaling its applied AI team **5× in 2026** to meet enterprise demand.
- **MLOps Engineer** — operational backbone of production AI. Growth of **9.8× over five years**, one of the steepest sustained climbs in the technology labor market.
- **Data Annotator / Model Evaluator** — one of the highest-volume new role categories, with global demand growing **25–35% annually**. Compensation ranges from $20–$40/hr for individual contributors to $150K for senior evaluation leads. These roles are the human infrastructure that makes AI systems reliable.
- **AI Systems Auditor / AI Quality Assurance Tester** — demand is spiking ahead of the EU AI Act's August 2 enforcement deadline, which mandates pre-deployment testing for all employment-related AI systems.

**New this week:** the emergence of the AI Enablement Lead signals a maturation point. Previous waves created builders (engineers) and watchers (auditors). This wave is creating internal change agents — people whose job is to make AI adoption actually work across an organization. That's a role displaced workers with strong operational and people skills are well-positioned to pivot into.

## Skills in Demand

AI fluency has crossed from differentiator to baseline. **75% of US tech job postings** now include AI skill requirements, and **1 in 10 postings across all sectors** explicitly require AI skills — triple the rate in 2023.

- **Agentic AI and agent frameworks** dominate the growth charts. YoY posting growth by sub-skill: Enterprise Integration **+638%**, Agentic AI **+587%**, AI Agents **+503%**, Responsible AI **+495%**, AI Infrastructure **+366%**, Vector Databases **+353%**.
- **Prompt engineering** roles grew **135% YoY**, with salary bands of **$90K–$250K**. But the bar has risen: hiring managers now expect prompt engineers to know eval design, observability, governance stacks, and MCP integration — not just "write good prompts."
- **The AI skills wage premium is substantial.** Workers with advanced AI skills earn **56% more** than peers in the same roles without them. The AI talent demand-to-supply ratio stands at **3.2-to-1** globally (1.6M open positions vs. 518K qualified candidates).
- **Soft skills are surging alongside technical ones.** LinkedIn reports that **7 of the top 10** fastest-growing skill categories are human-centric: conflict mitigation, public speaking, leadership, executive communication. **62% of hiring managers** say technical and soft skills are equally important; **24% say soft skills now matter more**.
- **The skills gap is widening.** 62% of hiring managers say the gap is more pronounced than a year ago. Only **6% of organizations** say they have the talent needed for high-priority projects (Deloitte). IDC projects the global AI skills gap threatens **$5.5 trillion** in unrealized market value.
- **Certifications are booming** — the global certification market is projected to grow from $54.5B (2026) to $88.2B by 2035 — but the market is shifting toward valuing **certifications + practical labs + hands-on projects** over credentials alone. 32% of failed AI hires in 2026 resulted from candidates with impressive resumes but outdated hands-on skills.

**The takeaway on skills:** the fastest path to the AI wage premium remains building, not studying. A portfolio of hands-on work with agentic frameworks, RAG pipelines, or evaluation tooling outperforms a stack of certificates. But don't sleep on the soft skills — the organizations deploying AI need people who can communicate across technical and business teams, and that's a skill set many displaced workers already have.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

July 2026 may be the most consequential month for workforce policy since the original WIOA passage. Multiple federal and state actions are converging.

- **Workforce Pell Grants launched July 1** — the Department of Education expanded Pell Grant eligibility to short-term training programs (as short as **8 weeks / 150 clock hours**) leading to recognized credentials in IT, healthcare, skilled trades, and early childhood education. Previously, Pell required programs of at least 15 weeks. This is a landmark expansion of federal financial aid for career changers. Caveat: only 12 states had created application road maps for colleges as of late June — access will be uneven initially.
- **Senator Warner unveiled a comprehensive AI legislative framework (July 21)** including a **National Workforce Transition Fund** that would dedicate revenue from limiting bonus depreciation for AI data center infrastructure to fund worker retraining, individual training accounts, employer retention grants, and tuition assistance. Also includes the bipartisan **Economy of the Future Commission Act** (with Sen. Rounds) to develop solutions for AI-driven workforce changes.
- **DOL announced $162M in apprenticeship expansion grants (July 7)** across five cooperative agreements targeting shipbuilding, defense, telecom, IT, and automotive services, with a goal of surpassing 1 million active apprentices.
- **The No Robot Bosses Act** was introduced in both the Senate and House (June 18), prohibiting employers from predominantly relying on automated decision systems for hiring, firing, scheduling, and discipline. California's parallel **SB 947 "No Robo Bosses Act of 2026"** passed the Senate 29–9 and is now before the Assembly. Together, they signal a regulatory wave around algorithmic management.
- **The EU AI Act's high-risk obligations take effect August 2** — any AI system used in recruitment, performance monitoring, or termination decisions must comply with mandatory risk assessments, bias testing, human oversight, and transparency disclosures. Penalties: up to EUR 15M or 3% of global turnover. This applies to US companies serving EU markets and is driving urgent demand for AI auditors and compliance specialists.
- **Maine's employee electronic monitoring law took effect July 14** — requires written notice before monitoring digital activity and prohibits audiovisual monitoring in employee residences. Part of a broader wave: at least five states (California, Illinois, Texas, Colorado, Maine) enacted AI-specific employment regulations in 2026.
- **The Stronger Workforce for America Act (H.R. 8210)** is advancing through the House, reauthorizing WIOA and transferring adult education funding from Education to Labor to better align workforce programs with employer needs.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

This week crystallizes a paradox that defines the 2026 labor market: **companies are cutting headcount while reporting strong revenue, and the same technology driving displacement is creating a parallel economy of new roles that didn't exist 18 months ago.**

Visa, Monday.com, and Patreon didn't lay people off because they were failing — they did it because AI made it possible to do the same work with fewer people. That's a harder story to navigate than a downturn, because there's no recovery to wait for. The efficiency gains are permanent. But the flip side is real too: 90,000 agentic AI job postings, a 56% wage premium for AI skills, and a 3.2-to-1 demand-to-supply ratio for AI talent. The bottleneck isn't jobs — it's the bridge between the old roles and the new ones.

**If you're navigating a transition:** the Workforce Pell expansion is the single biggest new on-ramp this month. Eight-week training programs in IT, healthcare, and skilled trades are now eligible for federal financial aid — no four-year degree required. Pair that with the $162M in DOL apprenticeship funding, and there's more publicly funded transition infrastructure than at any point in the AI era. But don't wait for your state to set up application road maps — only 12 have done so. Call your local community college's financial aid office directly.

**If you're building skills:** the market is clear — hands-on experience with agentic frameworks beats credentials. But the soft-skills signal is just as important and underreported. Seven of LinkedIn's top 10 fastest-growing skills are human-centric (conflict mitigation, leadership, communication). If you're coming from operations, customer service, or management, those skills are already in your toolkit. The move is to pair them with enough AI fluency to speak both languages.

**If you're watching policy:** Senator Warner's National Workforce Transition Fund and the No Robot Bosses Act represent two very different approaches — one builds on-ramps, the other builds guardrails. Both are needed. And the EU AI Act's August 2 deadline is creating immediate demand for compliance and audit skills that US workers can fill. The regulatory wave isn't just a constraint on employers — it's a job creator.

The displacement is real and accelerating. But the infrastructure — federal funding, new role categories, policy guardrails — is building faster than it was three months ago. The gap is in awareness and access, not in opportunity. Close that gap.
