# Market Pulse — Week of July 24, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date layoffs have reached **205,832 workers** across **322 events** — roughly 1,009 job losses per day since January 1. **54% of layoff events** now explicitly cite AI, automation, or machine learning as a factor, up from the roughly 50% share tracked in April.

- **Monday.com** cut **620 employees (20% of its global workforce)** on July 22, calling it the largest product transformation in company history. Leadership framed the layoffs as a deliberate pivot to an "AI-driven growth strategy" — not a response to revenue trouble. 350 of the cuts are in Israel. Restructuring charges estimated at $45–55M.
- **Microsoft** cut **4,800 jobs (2.1% of headcount)** on July 6. Roughly 1,600 hit the Xbox division, with gaming reductions expected to reach 3,200 (20% of Xbox) this fiscal year. Commercial sales teams were also cut. The stock is down 19% YTD as investors weigh whether AI will erode enterprise software revenue faster than it creates new lines.
- **Samsung** filed WARN notices for **918 cuts** in New Jersey alone — 739 tied to a headquarters relocation from Englewood Cliffs to Plano, TX, plus 179 at Samsung SDS.
- **Novartis** announced **572 cuts** in NJ (322 effective by October, on top of 250 announced March–May). **Verizon** added **282 more** at Basking Ridge. New Jersey has seen nearly **12,000 layoffs** since January — 3,500 more than the same period in 2025.
- **Federal government**: the workforce shrank by **256,000 positions (11%+)** from December 2024 to January 2026 across 22 major agencies. **OPM** alone cut **35%** of its headcount and is pursuing further reductions. In July, OPM finalized a rule centralizing certain federal firing decisions and launched another round of deferred resignations.

**The paradox of the month:** despite all of this, initial unemployment claims fell to **187,000** for the week ending July 23 — the **lowest level since 1969**. Corporate restructuring is real, but displaced workers are finding new positions faster than the layoff headlines suggest. The labor market isn't breaking — it's reorganizing.

## Emerging Roles

The role landscape is splitting into three lanes: agentic AI builders, AI infrastructure, and human-AI coordination.

- **Agentic AI Engineer / Multi-Agent Systems Engineer** — designs autonomous agents that reason, plan, and execute multi-step tasks. Postings mentioning "agentic AI" grew **986%** between 2023 and 2024 and are still accelerating. Box, McKinsey, and LinkedIn all flag this as the single fastest-growing job family.
- **Agent Orchestration Specialist** — deploys and manages fleets of AI agents, sets guardrails, monitors performance. An operations role, not a research one — the production counterpart to the agentic engineer.
- **AI Solutions Architect** — growth rate **+109.3%** YoY. Bridges technical AI capabilities and business use cases. The role that decides whether an agent, a copilot, or a traditional workflow is the right tool.
- **AI Content Creator** — growth rate **+134.5%** YoY. Not traditional content marketing — these roles use generative AI to produce, curate, and QA content pipelines at scale.
- **AI Product Manager** — growth rate **+89.7%** YoY. Manages roadmaps for AI-native products where the "feature" is a model behavior, not a UI widget.
- **Data center electrical and maintenance workers** — data center postings have **more than doubled** over two years. 6 in every 1,000 US job postings are now data center roles, up from 2 per 1,000 in May 2023. A quarter of that hiring is for electricians, installers, and maintenance techs — blue-collar AI-era work.
- **AI Systems Auditor / AI Governance Specialist** — early-stage but growing as enterprises and regulators both demand pre-deployment testing and compliance documentation.

**What's new this week:** the Indeed Hiring Lab's July 8 report confirms that **37% of the recent software-development posting recovery** came from roles with "AI" in the title. The rebound isn't broad — it's concentrated in AI-native positions. Software postings climbed ~15% since late February 2025, but 71% of the gain was in senior roles. Mid-level and junior software positions remain flat.

## Skills in Demand

The signal from job postings and compensation data is sharpening: AI-adjacent skills command a growing premium, but distinctly human capabilities are rising alongside them — not in spite of them.

- **AI engineering and implementation** remains the #1 fastest-growing skill cluster per LinkedIn's 2026 Skills on the Rise report. Prompt engineering, LLM development, RAG, MLOps, and agent frameworks are the core technical stack.
- **The AI salary premium is now quantified at 56%.** Workers with demonstrated AI expertise command salaries more than half again higher than peers in equivalent roles without AI skills (Mercor, CertiProf 2026 analyses).
- **Skills in AI-exposed jobs are changing 2× faster** than in least-exposed jobs (PwC 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, analyzing 1B+ job ads across 6 continents). The implication: reskilling isn't a one-time event — it's a continuous practice.
- **Entry-level roles are being "seniorised."** Openings for entry-level positions with higher-than-traditional skill expectations grew **35% since 2019**, while standard entry-level postings shrank 10% (PwC). AI handles the rote work that used to train juniors; employers now expect new hires to arrive with judgment.
- **Human-centered skills are not a consolation prize.** New tasks added to AI-exposed roles are **2.5× more likely** to require empathy, judgment, and creativity than tasks in non-exposed roles (PwC). Resilience, adaptability, and analytical thinking round out the WEF's top differentiators between growing and declining occupations.
- **AI business strategy and responsible AI** — the non-technical bridge between AI teams and leadership. Operational efficiency, executive communication, and risk compliance all appear in LinkedIn's top 8 fastest-growing skill categories alongside the technical AI stack.

**The gap to close:** 94% of leaders report AI-critical skill shortages, and 1 in 3 say those gaps exceed 40% of their workforce. Only 34% of organizations see more than half their employees actively engaging with upskilling programs. The demand is clear; the pipeline is not.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **"A Stronger Workforce for America Act" (H.R. 8210)** — introduced April 6, passed the House Education and Workforce Committee on April 21 along party lines (19–14). The bill reauthorizes WIOA, aligns workforce programs with employer needs, and expands apprenticeship pathways. It has not yet reached the full House floor and faces a steep Senate climb due to partisan splits.
- **OPM "Merit Hiring Plan" (May 2026)** — sets goals for reducing federal time-to-hire and shifting to skills-based recruitment. A structural signal: even the government is moving toward hiring for demonstrated capability over credentials.
- **Federal 4-to-1 replacement ratio** — the hiring freeze has hardened into a permanent constraint. Agencies can hire one person for every four departures, enforced through Strategic Hiring Committees and mandatory Annual Staffing Plans. This isn't temporary austerity — it's the new operating model.
- **Only 17% of workers displaced by automation** currently receive government-funded retraining support (SHRM). 56% of displaced workers in automation-heavy sectors report difficulty transitioning to new roles. The policy infrastructure is not keeping pace with the displacement rate.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

This week's data tells two stories at once — and both are true.

**Story one: AI restructuring is accelerating.** Monday.com didn't cut 20% of its workforce because it was struggling — it did it because it's betting its future on AI-native product architecture. That's the pattern now. Companies are cutting in strength, not weakness, and 54% are naming AI as the reason. When a profitable SaaS company voluntarily eliminates a fifth of its people to "transform," the restructuring wave has moved past the defensive phase into the strategic one.

**Story two: the labor market is absorbing the displaced faster than expected.** Jobless claims at a 57-year low while 200,000+ workers have been cut this year means re-employment is happening — just not in the same roles or sectors. The Indeed data confirms it: software postings are recovering, but almost entirely in AI-titled positions and senior roles. The floor is rising. Entry-level roles are being seniorised. The bar for re-entry is higher than it was a year ago.

**If you're navigating a transition:** the 56% AI salary premium is real and accessible. You don't need a degree to close the gap — hands-on experience with one agentic framework, one RAG pipeline, or one agent orchestration project is what hiring managers are scanning for. The data center hiring boom is also worth watching if you're open to infrastructure work — electricians and maintenance techs are in acute demand, and the roles pay well.

**If you're early-career:** the "seniorisation" trend is the one to take seriously. Standard entry-level roles are shrinking while entry-plus-AI-skills openings grow. Build a portfolio that shows you can work alongside AI tools, not just use them passively. That's the new floor.

The restructuring isn't slowing down. But the workers who move fastest — toward AI-adjacent skills, toward the new role categories, toward hands-on portfolios — are landing. The on-ramps exist. Use them.
