# Market Pulse — Week of October 28, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date tech layoffs have passed **196,000 workers** across hundreds of companies, per TrueUp's tracker as of October 23. October alone has logged **13,400+ cuts across 34 companies** through the 12th, with the full month likely higher. The overall labor market picture is paradoxical: initial jobless claims hit a 57-year low of 189,000 in July, yet hiring has slowed to a crawl — a freeze-in-place dynamic rather than a mass-layoff wave.

- **Barclays** is cutting roughly **5,000 jobs** as it leans further on AI, with 1,000 already gone and 4,000 more expected by year-end. Banking's AI-driven restructuring is accelerating.
- **Netflix** cut approximately **1,000 employees (~6% of global workforce)** in October — a notable contraction for a company that was aggressively hiring as recently as 2024.
- **HubSpot** laid off **~660 workers (7% of staff)**, joining the growing list of SaaS companies trimming headcount even as revenue holds.
- **eBay** plans to eliminate about **1,000 positions (~9% of workforce)**, continuing the e-commerce sector's post-pandemic right-sizing.
- **Kroger** announced plans to cut **3,000 corporate employees** over three years as it ramps up AI deployment in operations and logistics.

**What's different this week:** the displacement is no longer concentrated in tech. Banking (Barclays), retail (Kroger), and entertainment (Netflix) are all making AI-attributed cuts in the same month. Meanwhile, the 24/7 Wall St. analysis flags that the bigger story may not be layoffs but the **hiring freeze** — companies aren't shedding at historic rates, but they've stopped adding. For job seekers, that means fewer open doors even in a statistically tight market. Entry-level and junior roles are the sharpest casualty: CBS News reports a **16% drop in employment for workers aged 22–25** in AI-exposed roles (citing Goldman Sachs).

## Emerging Roles

The role landscape continues to mature from experimental AI titles into operational and governance positions:

- **AI Governance & Ethics Specialist** — companies formalizing responsible-AI policies are hiring dedicated compliance and bias-mitigation roles. As regulatory frameworks tighten (see Policy section), this role is shifting from "nice to have" to mandatory for enterprises deploying AI at scale.
- **AI Enablement Lead / AI Transformation Lead** — business-side titles that coordinate an organization's AI adoption strategy. Not technical builders — they sit between C-suite strategy and the teams doing implementation.
- **MLOps / AI Infrastructure Engineer** — the operational backbone of production AI. Companies that deployed models in 2024–2025 are now hiring to keep them reliable and cost-efficient. One of the most consistently cited roles across multiple hiring analyses.
- **AI Model Evaluator / Human Trainer** — demand for people who evaluate, red-team, and fine-tune AI outputs is growing **25–35% annually** per industry estimates. The role spans from annotation work to expert-level domain evaluation.
- **Chief AI Officer** — now common at Fortune 500 companies. The title has crossed from early-adopter tech firms into financial services, healthcare, and manufacturing.
- **Data Centre Technician** — the physical infrastructure boom behind AI is driving demand for hands-on technical roles in facility operations, cooling systems, and hardware deployment.

**The pattern:** earlier waves of AI hiring created specialist engineering roles. This wave is creating **management, governance, and infrastructure roles** — signals that AI deployment is moving from pilot to production across industries. The AI Enablement Lead, in particular, is an accessible entry point for experienced operations and project management professionals looking to pivot.

## Skills in Demand

The Bipartisan Policy Center's September 2026 analysis of Lightcast data is the headline: **US job postings listing AI skills are up 165% year over year.** Stanford's 2026 AI Index puts AI-related skills in about 2.5% of all US job postings — a 297% increase over the past decade. But the demand picture extends well beyond technical AI skills.

- **AI engineering and agentic frameworks** — hands-on experience with LLM orchestration, RAG pipelines, vector databases, and agent deployment remains the hottest skill cluster. Demand is no longer concentrated in tech: the BPC's July analysis found AI skill requirements spreading rapidly into professional services, healthcare, and government.
- **People management (+40.1%) and account management (+32.8%)** are among the fastest-growing skills in Q3 2026 per SHRM's quarterly review — a reminder that human coordination skills are rising alongside, not despite, AI adoption.
- **Critical thinking, adaptability, and communication** — Upwork's 2026 analysis calls these "power skills" and reports they're increasingly listed as explicit requirements, not assumed. Teams working with AI agents need people who can exercise judgment, escalate, and course-correct.
- **Cost accounting (+26.5%) and CRM (+26.6%)** round out SHRM's fast-movers — operational and financial skills that remain essential as companies restructure around AI.
- **Skills-based hiring hits 70%.** NACE's Job Outlook 2026 reports that 70% of employers now use skills-based hiring, up from 65% last year. The credential-to-portfolio shift continues.
- **The wage premium is substantial.** PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer puts the average wage premium for AI skills at **62%**. The fastest path to that premium remains hands-on project work, not certifications alone.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

Several policy changes took effect or advanced in October, with direct implications for displaced workers and career changers:

- **OPM eliminates time-in-grade restriction** — On October 19, OPM published a final rule removing the time-in-grade requirement for competitive-service GS advancement, effective January 19, 2027. This could open faster advancement paths for federal employees and make government careers more attractive to mid-career switchers.
- **OFCCP rescinds EO 11246 regulations** — effective October 26, OFCCP is rescinding the regulations implementing Executive Order 11246 (federal contractor affirmative action requirements). The practical impact on workforce composition at federal contractors remains to be seen.
- **USCIS eliminates automatic work permit extensions** — finalized October 14, this affects immigrant workers previously eligible for automatic extensions. For employers, it creates potential workforce gaps; for affected workers, it adds urgency to status renewals.
- **Wagner-Peyser staffing flexibility** — a rule taking effect October 19 lets states choose how they staff Employment Service offices, potentially reshaping the frontline of public workforce services.
- **Workforce Pell enacted** — one of the most significant federal workforce funding changes, expanding Pell Grant eligibility to shorter-term training programs. This directly lowers the financial barrier for displaced workers seeking rapid reskilling.
- **Continuing Resolution through December 11** — federal workforce programs operate under flat funding until then. No new workforce investment money until a full appropriations deal.
- **U.K. employment status reform consultation** — launched October 14, closing January 8, 2027. The UK is reconsidering how it classifies the self-employed — relevant context for the growing gig and contract workforce globally.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

**The hiring freeze is the displacement signal hiding in plain sight.** October's layoff headlines — Barclays, Netflix, Kroger, eBay — matter, but the deeper challenge is the one 24/7 Wall St. flagged: companies aren't cutting at historic rates, but they've stopped hiring at normal rates. Jobless claims are near record lows. Hiring is near a standstill. For anyone in transition, that means the competition for each open role is fiercer than the topline numbers suggest.

The opportunity side is equally real. AI skill demand is up 165% year over year and spreading beyond tech into services, healthcare, and government. The wage premium for AI skills is 62%. New roles in AI governance, enablement, and infrastructure are creating accessible entry points for experienced professionals who aren't engineers. And Workforce Pell is now law — shorter-term training programs can be funded through federal financial aid for the first time.

**If you're navigating a transition:** the market rewards builders over credential-collectors. Pick one AI-adjacent skill area that connects to your existing experience — AI enablement if you're in operations, AI governance if you're in compliance or legal, MLOps if you're in IT infrastructure — and build a small portfolio project. That hands-on proof matters more than a certificate. Check whether your state has updated Wagner-Peyser services or expanded training eligibility under Workforce Pell.

**If you're early-career and feeling the freeze:** entry-level hiring in AI-exposed roles is genuinely down. Focus on demonstrable skills, not applications volume. AI model evaluation and training is one of the fastest-growing entry points and doesn't require an engineering degree — it requires domain knowledge, attention to detail, and the ability to assess output quality.

The labor market is in a strange limbo: low layoffs, low hiring, high restructuring. The organizations that survive this period will look different on the other side. Position yourself for what they're building toward, not what they're cutting away from.
