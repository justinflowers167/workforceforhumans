# Market Pulse — Week of June 25, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date layoffs have reached **185,894 workers across 267 events** — averaging **1,062 job losses per day**, outpacing the 2025 pace that ended at ~245,000 for the full year. **56% of events now explicitly cite AI, automation, or machine learning** as a driving factor, affecting 156,270 workers across 150 companies.

- **Elastic** cut **~7% of its workforce** (June 23) to "realign investments for the age of AI automation." CPO Ken Exner also departed. The company expects $22–25M in severance charges — but says total headcount will still grow this fiscal year via strategic rehiring in customer-facing and AI roles. The cut-then-rehire pattern is becoming a template: shed generalist roles, backfill with AI-aligned ones.
- **iHeartMedia** launched a new wave of **programming layoffs across dozens of markets** (June 23–24), part of a $150M annualized cost-cutting campaign. On-air talent and programming staff are bearing the brunt as the company frames the restructuring around "technology-driven" programming. Media and broadcasting continue to hollow out mid-tier creative-operations roles.
- **ServiceNow** laid off **hundreds of employees** (June 12, still reverberating) across solution consulting, sales, product marketing, and learning & development — citing the same AI efficiencies it sells to its own clients. A sharp reversal from the company's 2023 no-layoff pledge.
- **GitLab** is still executing its **14% workforce reduction (~350 employees)** announced June 3, explicitly framing the move as a pivot to the "agentic era." The company is exiting 22 countries, flattening up to three management layers, and partnering with an AI lab to rebuild its infrastructure for agent workloads. Revenue is up 23% YoY — the cuts aren't about survival, they're about architectural transformation.
- **Microsoft** — employees on Blind report **quiet, undisclosed cuts** via direct firings and voluntary-separation packages (June 19 onward), potentially bypassing WARN Act thresholds. Larger formal cuts are rumored for July 2026.
- **Tennessee** saw a **cluster of four plant closures** (WARN notices filed week of June 18–22): Pilgrim's Pride Chattanooga (315), JBS Memphis (208), YesCare Brentwood (150), WK Kellogg Memphis (117) — totaling ~790 jobs in food manufacturing, healthcare, and CPG. A reminder that displacement isn't confined to tech.

**What's different this week:** the "cut and rehire" model is crystallizing. Elastic, ServiceNow, and GitLab are all reducing headcount while simultaneously hiring for AI-native roles — the displacement and creation are happening inside the same companies, in the same quarter. For workers in the affected roles, the message is blunt: the org chart is being redrawn around AI, and the new seats require different skills.

## Emerging Roles

The AI job market continues to specialize and stratify. US job postings requiring AI skills grew **144% year-over-year** as of April 2026, and the global economy has added **1.3 million new AI-related jobs** in two years — a pace no prior technology cycle has matched.

- **Agentic AI Engineer** — the breakout role of 2026. Job postings grew **280% YoY to ~90,000 US listings** (Stanford 2026 AI Index). These engineers design autonomous AI systems that reason, call tools, and execute multi-step workflows. Salaries average ~$190K, with top earners clearing $300K+. Deloitte, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Scale AI are all actively hiring.
- **AI Reliability Engineer** — the SRE equivalent for model behavior. Monitors production model performance, sets anomaly alerts, and ensures AI pipelines run predictably. Showing up in SaaS, fintech, and healthcare — anywhere models touch customers.
- **AI Governance Manager / Trust Engineer** — driven by regulatory pressure (Colorado's new ADMT framework, the EU AI Act, the proposed Great American AI Act). Finance and healthcare are hiring fastest. Trust Engineers embed fairness and safety constraints into AI systems from inception rather than auditing after deployment.
- **AI Enablement Lead** — runs internal adoption programs: employee training, playbook development, governance coordination, and usage tracking. A non-technical role that bridges the gap between the AI team and the rest of the organization. The title is new; the function is becoming standard at enterprises past the pilot phase.
- **Data Center Specialist** — the physical-infrastructure role powering the AI buildout. Employment projected to reach **650,000 jobs by 2026**, with an estimated **340,000 positions going unfilled**. The talent gap is acute and growing.
- **Forward-Deployed AI Engineer** — a hybrid engineering-consulting role implementing AI at customer sites. Listings rose **800%+ YoY**. Anthropic is scaling its applied AI team 5x in 2026.

**New this week:** the "AI Enablement Lead" and "AI Reliability Engineer" titles signal that AI adoption has passed the build-it phase and entered the run-it-and-scale-it phase. When companies hire people to keep models working reliably and to train the rest of the org to actually use them, the technology is no longer a project — it's infrastructure.

## Skills in Demand

The wage premium for AI skills continues to widen. PwC's June 15 AI Jobs Barometer shows AI-skilled roles growing **8x faster than the overall market**, with a **62% wage premium** over comparable non-AI positions — up from 56% last year and 25% the year before that.

- **Agentic AI and orchestration** — hands-on experience building multi-agent systems, tool-calling architectures, and reasoning pipelines is the single hottest skill cluster. The shift from "prompt engineering" to "context engineering" reflects this: the job is now about designing retrieval, memory, and execution context for autonomous agents, not writing better prompts.
- **Prompt engineering skills are being absorbed**, not eliminated. Postings mentioning prompt engineering are up 3x in 2026, but the title is migrating into broader role descriptions. The standalone "prompt engineer" is giving way to AI engineers, product managers, and designers who all prompt fluently — it's becoming a baseline competency rather than a specialty.
- **Cybersecurity** remains critically undersupplied — the skills-deficiency rate climbed to **44%**, with median salaries at $135K across 2,149 tracked roles. Every AI deployment creates new attack surface, and the talent isn't keeping up.
- **MLOps and cloud deployment** — "cloud familiarity" no longer clears ATS filters. Employers want hands-on experience with model serving, pipeline orchestration, monitoring, and cost optimization. MLOps saw **9.8x growth** over five years on LinkedIn's Emerging Jobs index.
- **Human-centered skills are rising, not falling.** 63% of employers now explicitly prioritize critical thinking, creativity, and emotional intelligence in postings. BCG reports that the new tasks being added to AI-reshaped roles are **2.5x more likely to require empathy, judgment, and creativity** than the tasks being automated away. The floor for human skills is going up, not down.

**The access point is shifting.** The fastest path to the AI wage premium remains portfolio-based, not credential-based. Short-term training programs are getting a major boost: Workforce Pell Grants open to 8–15 week programs starting July 1 (see Policy below). The barrier to entry has never been lower for someone willing to build and document hands-on work.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **Great American AI Act (GAAIA) — discussion draft released June 4.** The first comprehensive federal AI framework, introduced bipartisan by Rep. Obernolte (R-CA) and Rep. Trahan (D-MA). Title II covers workforce directly: employers must give **60 days' notice when AI is a "substantial factor" in mass layoffs**, including disclosure of the AI involved and pre-layoff retraining efforts. Also creates an AI Workforce Research Hub inside DOL and directs BLS/Census to update surveys to capture AI adoption data. Still a discussion draft — but the workforce-notice provision would be the most significant transparency requirement since WARN.
- **Workforce Pell Grants — launching July 1, 2026.** Final rule from the Department of Education opens Pell Grant eligibility to **8–15 week short-term training programs** for the first time. Targets skilled trades, healthcare, transportation, and tech. This is a material change: workers in transition can now use federal financial aid for bootcamp-length programs without committing to a full semester or degree.
- **Schedule Policy/Career executive order — signed June 3.** Reclassifies ~8,000 senior federal positions (97% GS-15+) as at-will, stripping standard civil-service job protections. Union coalitions (AFGE, AFSCM, AFL-CIO) have filed legal challenges. For the WFH audience, this matters because it accelerates the federal government's shift toward contract and AI-augmented staffing models at senior levels.
- **EU AI Act simplification — Parliament approved June 16.** Postpones high-risk AI system obligations from August 2026 to December 2027 (16-month deferral) and extends SME exemptions to companies up to 500 employees. For US-based workers, the signal is that European regulatory deadlines are softening, which may slow the creation of compliance-focused roles in the near term.
- **NSF TechAccess: AI-Ready America — Round 1 deadline passed June 16.** The $224M program will fund up to 56 coordination hubs (one per state/territory) for community colleges and universities to build local AI workforce capacity. First 10 hubs will be selected from this round. The infrastructure play: bringing AI training to community colleges, not just research universities.
- **Colorado AI Act replaced.** SB 24-205 never took effect — its June 30 start date was overtaken by SB 26-189 (signed May 14), which replaces the framework with a narrower "automated decision-making technology" regulation effective January 1, 2027. Employers using AI in hiring get a reprieve on compliance timelines but should prepare for the new ADMT requirements.
- **WIOA reauthorization advancing.** The "Stronger Workforce for America Act of 2026" passed the House Education & Workforce Committee in April with 50% of Adult/Dislocated Worker funding earmarked for training. Faces a filibuster hurdle in the Senate. If passed, this would be the most significant update to federal workforce training infrastructure since 2014.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

This week's signals converge on a single theme: **the cut-and-rehire economy is now the operating model, not an exception.**

Elastic, ServiceNow, and GitLab aren't shrinking — they're reshaping. They're shedding roles built for a pre-AI org chart and backfilling with positions that didn't exist two years ago. iHeartMedia is doing the same in broadcasting. The displacement and creation are happening simultaneously, inside the same companies. That's the new pattern: your employer might lay you off and post your replacement with a different title in the same quarter.

The policy response is catching up. The Great American AI Act's 60-day AI-layoff notice provision — if it survives the legislative process — would be the first federal requirement to name AI as a displacement factor. Workforce Pell Grants opening to short-term programs on July 1 removes a real barrier for people who can't afford to stop working for a full semester. And the NSF's state-by-state AI training hubs are an infrastructure bet that AI skills shouldn't be the province of coastal tech centers alone.

**If you're navigating a transition:** the Workforce Pell Grant expansion on July 1 is actionable now. An 8–15 week program in AI fundamentals, cybersecurity, or MLOps — paid for with federal aid — is the kind of credential that maps directly to the roles companies are hiring for this quarter. The 62% wage premium for AI skills isn't theoretical; it's showing up in the postings today. Build something, document it, and make it findable.

**If you're currently employed but watching the shifts:** the "AI Enablement Lead" signal is your cue. Companies are hiring people to teach the rest of the org how to work with AI. If you can run that function inside your current organization — even informally — you're building the skill set for one of the fastest-growing role categories while making yourself harder to cut. Don't wait for the title; do the work.

The displacement numbers are real — 1,062 jobs a day, 56% citing AI. But the construction is real too: 1.3 million new AI roles in two years, a 62% wage premium, and federal money starting to flow into short-term training at scale. The gap between the off-ramp and the on-ramp is narrowing. The question is whether you're building toward the new roles or waiting for the old ones to come back. They're not coming back.
