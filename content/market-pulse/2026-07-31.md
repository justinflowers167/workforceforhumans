# Market Pulse — Week of July 31, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date tech layoffs have crossed **170,500 workers** across roughly **480 companies** — averaging **832 job losses per day** since January 1. AI has overtaken all other cited reasons for cuts, accounting for **101,743 layoffs in H1 2026 alone** (23% of all announced reductions). Analysts at Deutsche Bank have flagged "AI redundancy washing" — companies using AI as cover for strategic restructurings that would have happened regardless.

- **Microsoft** cut **4,800 jobs (2.1% of headcount)** on July 6 to open its fiscal year — a pattern that's become an annual July restructuring. Xbox was hardest hit (3,200 roles, 20% of the division), with additional thousands cut across sales and consulting. CPO Amy Coleman cited AI as changing "how work gets done." This followed an April voluntary-retirement buyout that ~2,600 eligible workers accepted, softening the July RIF.
- **Monday.com** laid off **630 employees (20% of workforce)** on July 22, restructuring around its AI Work Platform where employees and AI agents collaborate. Management framed the cuts as an adaptation to the AI era, not cost savings — though the company still plans to hire in "key strategic areas." Expected restructuring charges: $45–55 million.
- **Intel** initiated a new round of layoffs targeting its **Data Center and AI Group** on July 21. The company didn't disclose numbers, but the cuts come on top of roughly 40,000 roles eliminated in 2024–2025. CEO Lip-Bu Tan is realigning the division for "long-term success" ahead of Q2 earnings.
- **Samsung** cut **839 U.S. jobs** (739 in Englewood Cliffs, NJ; ~100 in Plano, TX) as part of a headquarters relocation to Texas. Profits are surging on AI chip demand — the cuts hit sales and marketing while the company reinvests in semiconductor capacity.
- **Visa** cut **2,600 employees (7% of workforce)** on July 28 — primarily technology and product teams. CEO Ryan McInerney cited AI as reshaping "the way work gets done at Visa," reducing repetitive work and accelerating product development. Visa is reinvesting savings into stablecoin, cross-border, and B2B payment solutions.
- **Verizon** is selling **274 company-owned retail stores** (affecting ~2,500 retail workers) and cutting **~500 corporate positions** as part of an ongoing restructuring. Workers face transfer-or-separation decisions as stores shift to authorized retailers.
- **Entain** (gaming/betting) began a restructuring with **500 job cuts** on July 23, reflecting cost pressure from UK gambling tax increases and competitive pressure from prediction markets.
- **Freight and logistics** saw **1,222+ layoffs** across July from Amazon (494 temporary at a Florida fulfillment center closing for renovation), Temco Logistics (223 across three states), Freight Handlers Inc. (168 at Publix distribution centers), and cuts at GEODIS, CJ Logistics America, and GXO. Ten transportation/distribution firms filed Chapter 11.

**What's different this week:** Visa and Monday.com together tell the story. A payments giant cutting 7% of its tech workforce to fund AI infrastructure, and a mid-market SaaS company restructuring its entire operating model around human-AI agent teams — both framing the cuts as structural, not cyclical. "AI-native org design" has moved past the Fortune 500 early-adopter phase into the broader enterprise tier. Meanwhile, Microsoft's annual July restructuring is becoming as predictable as earnings season, normalizing cyclical AI-linked workforce resets.

## Emerging Roles

The AI role landscape continues to separate into two tracks: deep-build (engineering the systems) and deep-bridge (connecting those systems to human workflows).

- **AI Engineer** is the #1 fastest-growing job on LinkedIn for the second consecutive year, with posting growth at **+143%** YoY. The role has become the default entry point for companies standing up production AI systems.
- **MLOps Engineer** has seen **9.8× growth** over five years. Indeed currently lists over **5,500 open MLOps positions** in the U.S. with base salaries from $90K to $257K — the operational backbone role for companies that shipped models in 2024–2025 and now need to keep them running.
- **Agentic AI Specialist** — designs and manages autonomous AI workflows across enterprise systems. Compensation ranges £100K–£180K (~$125K–$225K) in current listings. Monday.com's restructuring is direct demand evidence: companies deploying agents need people who can architect the handoffs.
- **AI Content Creator** postings are up **+134.5%** — not traditional content roles repurposed, but hybrid positions requiring both creative judgment and AI-tool fluency for content generation, editing, and quality assurance.
- **AI Product Manager** roles are up **~90% YoY** — the bridge between technical AI teams and business outcomes. These roles require AI literacy plus traditional PM skills; the premium is in the combination.
- **AI Coach / AI Trainer** — demand for human evaluators and trainers of AI models is growing **25–35% annually** globally. These roles don't require deep engineering backgrounds, making them accessible on-ramps for career changers with domain expertise.
- **AI Compliance Manager** — governance and regulatory readiness roles are accelerating as enterprises face mounting audit and reporting requirements for AI deployments.

**New this week:** the AI Coach/Trainer growth trajectory is the signal for the WFH audience. Unlike most AI roles that require engineering backgrounds, these positions monetize domain expertise — a healthcare worker who can evaluate medical AI output, or a legal professional who can train contract-review models. The path from displaced professional to AI trainer is shorter than many assume.

## Skills in Demand

The skills market is splitting into a clear hierarchy: AI-native skills command the premium, but foundational human skills remain the floor.

- **AI and machine learning** now appear in **89% of job postings** across industries. A new Lightcast report found AI skills command a **28% salary premium ($18K/year average)**, rising to **43% for multi-skill holders**. Crucially, **51% of AI-skill job postings are now outside IT** — healthcare, finance, architecture, law — where the premiums are largest because the supply gap is widest.
- **AI video generation and editing** is the single fastest-growing skill at **+329%** — a niche signal pointing to the explosion of AI-generated media in marketing, entertainment, and corporate communications.
- **Data analytics** (SQL, Python, ML, statistics) shows **414% growth** — the steepest rise among foundational technical skill clusters.
- **Cybersecurity** will appear as a core skill requirement in **65% of technical job postings** by year-end — up from roughly 40% in 2025, driven by AI-generated attack surfaces.
- **Agentic frameworks and RAG architecture** — hands-on experience with orchestration tools, vector databases, and retrieval pipelines is now a baseline expectation for AI engineering roles, not a differentiator.
- **Human skills are holding firm**: seven of the top ten most-requested skills across all postings are soft skills — conflict resolution, communication, adaptability, leadership, and public speaking. The AI revolution hasn't displaced the need for people who can navigate ambiguity, build consensus, and lead teams through change.
- **AI literacy** has emerged as a standalone skill requirement across non-technical roles — HR, operations, finance, project management. Not "can you build a model" but "can you evaluate AI output and integrate AI tools into your workflow."

**The credential shift is structural.** A Lightcast survey found **71% of leaders now prefer less-experienced candidates with AI skills over more-experienced candidates without them**, and **45% of job postings prioritize skills over degrees**. The fastest path to the AI premium remains a portfolio of hands-on work, not a new credential.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **Workforce Pell Grants launched July 1** — the most significant federal workforce-training expansion in a decade. For the first time, Pell Grants can fund **short-term training programs (8–15 weeks)** that prepare students for immediate employment in high-skill, high-wage, or in-demand fields including IT, healthcare, skilled trades, and early childhood education. Previously, Pell eligibility required enrollment in degree or certificate programs of 600+ clock hours. This slashes the time-and-money barrier for displaced workers seeking rapid reskilling.
- **DOL Reskill Dislocated Worker Grants** — the Department of Labor announced **$50 million in funding** for Rapid Reskill Employment Recovery grants ($2M–$8M per award), targeting innovative models for reskilling dislocated workers in domestic manufacturing, aerospace, energy production, and construction. Applications due **August 3, 2026**.
- **National Skills Coalition budget warning** — NSC flagged the FY2026 budget proposal as "cuts disguised as reform," warning that proposed workforce-development funding reductions could undermine the very reskilling infrastructure the Workforce Pell expansion is meant to support. The tension between new access pathways and shrinking program budgets is worth watching.
- **WEF Reskilling Revolution** — the global initiative reports commitments expected to reach **856 million people by 2030**, with a new Skills Accelerator launching in India in 2026 alongside national action plans in Bahrain and Nigeria.
- **China's AI Agent Regulation** — China implemented the world's first binding regulatory framework focused entirely on AI agents the week of July 28, establishing a tiered decision-authorization system for agent autonomy. This will drive demand for AI compliance and governance professionals globally as other jurisdictions follow.
- **"A Stronger Workforce for America Act" (WIOA Reauthorization)** — advancing through Congress with a new **$65M Youth Apprenticeship Readiness Grant**, transfer of adult education oversight from the Department of Education to the DOL, and expanded workforce-board flexibility. Passed House Education and Workforce Committee in April; awaiting full House floor vote.
- **Canada's $450M workforce investment** — an additional $450 million over three years under Labour Market Development Agreements, focused on workers impacted by tariffs and global market shifts. A signal that workforce displacement from trade policy is running parallel to AI displacement.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

This week's most important signal isn't a layoff — it's the **Workforce Pell Grant going live on July 1**.

For the first time, federal financial aid covers 8-to-15-week training programs that lead directly to employment. That's a structural change in who can afford to reskill. If you're a displaced worker weighing whether you can take time for training, the math just shifted: Pell-eligible short-term programs in IT, healthcare, and skilled trades are now federally subsidized. Check your local community college or workforce board — many are already enrolling for fall cohorts.

Meanwhile, the displacement picture continues to broaden — Monday.com restructuring around AI agents, Microsoft's annual July reset, Intel trimming even its AI-adjacent divisions — but so does the on-ramp infrastructure. The $50M DOL reskilling grants (due August 3) are another lever, particularly for manufacturing and energy workers.

**The practical move this week:** if you're in transition or advising someone who is, look at the AI Coach/Trainer role family. It doesn't require an engineering background — it requires domain expertise and the ability to evaluate AI output in your field. Healthcare, legal, education, and finance professionals have transferable judgment that AI companies are actively hiring for, and the demand curve is 25–35% annually with no sign of flattening.

The workforce reorganization is real and accelerating. But the tools to navigate it are more accessible this month than they were last month. Use them.
