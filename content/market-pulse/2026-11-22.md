# Market Pulse — Week of November 22, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date tech layoffs have now crossed **200,000 jobs** across the industry, with November adding another 5,000+ confirmed cuts across 54 announcements so far. The pace has not meaningfully slowed since Q1.

- **Upwork** is planning to cut roughly **37% of its global workforce** — a stark signal from a company that built its business on the future of work. The freelancing platform's restructuring suggests that even labor-marketplace intermediaries aren't immune to the efficiency pressure they helped enable.
- **PepsiCo** is cutting approximately **1,000 North American jobs**, making consumer goods a growing source of displacement alongside tech. These are operations and corporate roles, not factory floor — the white-collar pattern continues.
- **Ubisoft** laid off **166 workers** across its Montreal and Quebec City studios this week. The gaming sector has been hammered throughout 2026, with Fortis Games (400), Schell Games (49, ~40% of staff), and others preceding this round.
- **Boeing** announced **2,500 cuts** as aerospace restructuring continues. **Qantas** cut **850 Australian roles** as part of an offshoring deal. **Vodafone Deutschland** is shedding **2,300 positions**.
- **Block** initiated another round of layoffs after eliminating more than 4,000 positions earlier this year. **Canva** cut roughly **1,200 jobs**, and **Qualcomm** eliminated **1,500 roles** in India.
- **Agriculture** surfaced an unexpected signal: a **Gebbers Farms WARN filing** covering approximately **3,400 workers** effective November 25 is the single largest US filing this month by headcount.

**What's different this week:** displacement is no longer sector-specific. Consumer goods (PepsiCo), telecom (Vodafone, Comcast), aerospace (Boeing), agriculture (Gebbers), and gaming (Ubisoft) are all cutting alongside tech. The common thread isn't AI alone — it's a compound of efficiency mandates, offshoring, and strategic restructuring, with AI providing the rationale or the tooling for all three.

## Emerging Roles

AI-native job categories continue to fragment and specialize. The most notable shift: governance and orchestration roles are catching up to pure engineering roles in posting volume.

- **AI Governance Lead / AI Auditor** — regulatory pressure from the EU AI Act and SEC AI disclosure requirements is converting what were ad-hoc compliance tasks into full-time positions. Organizations deploying AI at scale need someone accountable for bias, transparency, and regulatory reporting. These roles don't require deep ML knowledge — they need policy literacy, risk assessment, and cross-functional coordination.
- **Agent Orchestration Engineer** — designs and maintains multi-agent AI systems in production. Walmart, KPMG, and Salesforce are among the companies creating this title as they move from single-model pilots to complex agent workflows.
- **Human-AI Collaboration Designer** — an evolution of the "AI Workforce Manager" signal from earlier this year. These roles design the handoff protocols, escalation paths, and feedback loops between people and AI systems. Showing up in HR tech, consulting, and enterprise ops.
- **AI FinOps Specialist** — manages the unpredictable cost profiles of cloud AI deployments. As inference costs pile up, companies need someone who understands both the technical architecture and the financial controls.
- **AI Security Analyst** — focused specifically on adversarial attacks, prompt injection, data poisoning, and model supply-chain risks. A subspecialty of cybersecurity that barely existed 18 months ago.
- **Synthetic Data Curator / AI Trainer** — the human-in-the-loop roles that keep AI systems calibrated. Evaluation, red-teaming, and data quality work are growing as companies realize model performance degrades without ongoing human oversight.

**New this week:** the governance and audit roles signal a maturation point. When companies start hiring for compliance and risk around AI — not just building and deploying it — the technology has crossed from experimental to institutional. That's where the durable jobs tend to form.

## Skills in Demand

The Bipartisan Policy Center's latest analysis of Lightcast job-posting data provides the strongest signal this quarter: **AI-related skills in postings jumped 27% between Q4 2025 and August 2026**, on top of an earlier 8% rise — a compounding 165% increase year-over-year.

- **AI skills are accelerating but still niche in raw posting volume.** PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer found postings requiring specific AI skills grew **69%** year-over-year, versus 9% growth in the broader market. Yet Kickresume's scan of 1.4 million US/UK postings found explicit AI tool mentions in only **0.2%** of ads. The gap suggests AI fluency is increasingly assumed rather than listed — or that many employers haven't updated their posting templates yet.
- **Management and communication are the volume leaders.** Kickresume found management is the single most requested skill, appearing in **44% of job ads**. CodeSignal's analysis of 50 million postings puts communication and interpersonal skills at **16%**. These aren't new, but their persistence alongside AI demand confirms the "both/and" pattern: technical AI skills open doors, but human coordination skills keep you in the room.
- **Prompt engineering and RAG architecture** continue their rise as mid-skill-level entry points. They're learnable in weeks, not years, and are showing up in non-technical roles — marketing, operations, customer success — as AI tools permeate workflows.
- **AI product management and responsible AI** are the bridge roles between technical teams and business leadership. Demand is being pulled by enterprises that have deployed models but lack the organizational muscle to govern them.

**The wage premium is real and growing.** PwC's data shows AI-skilled workers commanding significantly higher compensation across sectors. The fastest path to that premium remains hands-on project work over credentials — a portfolio of real implementations beats a certificate wall.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **DOL proposes sweeping PERM overhaul (November 17)** — the Department of Labor released a proposed rule modernizing the permanent labor certification (green card) process. Key provisions: eliminating Schedule A (the pre-approved shortage occupation list), prohibiting PERM filings within **two years of a relevant layoff** affecting US workers in the same occupation, and updating prevailing wage methodology. Public comments open through January 19, 2027. For the WFH audience, the two-year layoff restriction is the headline: it would create a concrete incentive for employers to retain and retrain domestic workers before turning to overseas hiring.
- **Independent contractor rule finalized (November 19)** — DOL issued a final rule clarifying the classification test, with implications for gig workers and freelancers navigating AI-disrupted labor markets.
- **Joint employer rule under review** — sent to OIRA on November 13, this rule could reshape liability for staffing agencies and subcontracted work arrangements — directly relevant to displaced workers entering contingent roles.
- **Congressional shift on federal workforce** — Democrats' new congressional majority is positioning to push back on the administration's Schedule Policy/Career reclassification of roughly **8,000 federal positions**. Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-VA) is pushing for a federal pay raise in year-end funding legislation, countering the planned 2027 pay freeze.
- **McKinsey's "Workforce in Motion" report (September 2026)** — estimates that **25% of workers** could see more than 30% of their task time reshuffled or shifted to new activities. The IEEE's August workforce outlook added that AI skills are approaching **"nearly mandatory"** status for many white-collar roles.
- **Reskilling gap persists** — LHH's data shows only **36.9%** of workers laid off due to AI were reemployed within three months, compared to 46.2% for non-AI layoffs. Adecco found that while 75% of workers using AI tools reported higher productivity, only **25% had received any training** on applying AI at work. The gap between AI adoption and AI preparation continues to widen.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

The November signals confirm what the year has been building toward: **displacement has gone cross-sector, but the infrastructure to navigate it is finally catching up.**

The layoff pattern is no longer a "tech story." PepsiCo, Boeing, Vodafone, and Gebbers Farms are cutting alongside Canva and Block. The common denominator is structural efficiency pressure — AI-enabled, but not AI-exclusive. If you're in operations, corporate support, or mid-level management in any sector, the exposure is real and not hypothetical.

The opportunity side is equally concrete. Governance, audit, and orchestration roles are maturing from experimental titles into institutional positions. The DOL's proposed PERM restrictions would, if finalized, create a direct incentive for employers to invest in domestic reskilling rather than offshoring. And the skills data is unambiguous: AI fluency commands a wage premium, and the fastest path there is building, not studying.

**If you're navigating a transition:** the two skills worth investing in right now are prompt engineering (learnable in weeks, applicable across roles) and AI governance literacy (the compliance wave is just starting, and it favors people who understand both policy and technology). The DOL's PERM comment period is also worth watching — workforce advocates should weigh in before January 19.

**If you're mid-career and employed:** don't wait for a formal training program. Adecco's finding — 75% productivity gains among AI users but only 25% trained — means most organizations are leaving reskilling to individual initiative. That's a gap you can exploit. Build one AI workflow into your current role, document the results, and you've created your own proof of capability.

The displacement wave is broad. The on-ramps are narrower but real. Move toward them.
