# Market Pulse — Week of November 13, 2026

A weekly scan of workforce displacement and transformation signals. Grounded reading of where the market is tightening, where it's opening, and what to do next.

## Displacement Signals

Year-to-date tech layoffs have crossed **135,000 workers** across **334 companies** on layoffs.fyi's count — already surpassing all of 2025. A broader tracker (TradingPlatforms) puts the figure closer to **204,000** across 699 events. The gap reflects methodological differences, but the direction is unambiguous: 2026 is a heavier year than last.

- **AI is now the leading cited factor.** Depending on the tracker, between **36% and 56%** of 2026 tech layoffs are attributed to AI-driven restructuring. However, a November 5 deep-dive found that fewer than 1 in 4 of those "AI-tagged" cuts came with an actual company statement naming AI as the cause — the rest are analyst attributions. Real, but softer than the headline number implies.
- **Fiserv** cut **3,000 employees and contractors** under its new CEO (announced November 3). The financial-services infrastructure firm joins the growing list of non-tech companies applying tech-sector restructuring logic.
- **Schaeffler**, a major German automotive supplier, is cutting **3,500 jobs** and closing four German manufacturing sites — a reminder that AI-adjacent automation is hitting industrial supply chains, not just white-collar roles.
- **Ecco** is closing factories in Portugal and Vietnam, eliminating over **2,400 jobs** in a restructuring of its manufacturing footprint.
- **Canva** is reportedly weighing another round of redundancies or a hiring freeze this month, though the company hasn't confirmed. Worth watching — design tools are one of the categories most directly disrupted by generative AI.
- **WARN Act filings** project **28,844 scheduled layoffs** across the six weeks from November 3 through December 8, with the heaviest week (November 24) accounting for roughly 9,300 workers.

**What's different this week:** the displacement signal is no longer concentrated in tech. Fiserv (fintech infrastructure), Schaeffler (automotive parts), and Ecco (consumer goods manufacturing) show that the AI-efficiency playbook has fully crossed sector boundaries. The "tech layoff" framing is increasingly a misnomer — this is a cross-industry restructuring.

## Emerging Roles

The AI role landscape continues to specialize, with agent-related positions now dominating the growth charts:

- **Agentic AI Engineer** — demand up **260% YoY** in India and similarly elevated in the US. These roles build, deploy, and maintain autonomous AI agent systems. The title barely registered in job databases 18 months ago.
- **Agent Supervisor / AgentOps Engineer** — described in multiple 2026 analyses as roles that "didn't exist six months ago." Responsible for monitoring, debugging, and coordinating fleets of AI agents in production environments.
- **AI Compliance Specialist / Algorithm Auditor** — growing faster than general AI hiring, per LinkedIn data. Driven by the EU AI Act enforcement timeline, SEC disclosure expectations, and enterprise risk management. The governance layer is hiring in earnest.
- **AI FinOps Specialist** — manages the cost of running AI workloads at scale. As inference costs become a material line item for enterprises, this hybrid finance-engineering role is emerging to optimize spend.
- **Synthetic Data Curator** — creates and validates training datasets when real data is scarce, sensitive, or biased. Appearing at AI labs, pharma companies, and autonomous vehicle firms.
- **AI Security Analyst** — focuses on adversarial attacks, prompt injection, model poisoning, and data exfiltration risks specific to AI systems. A cybersecurity offshoot that's diverging into its own discipline.
- **Forward-Deployed AI Engineer** — the embed model continues to grow, with companies placing AI engineers directly inside customer organizations to tailor systems to enterprise-specific contexts.

**New this week:** the "AgentOps" cluster (agent supervisors, agent reliability engineers, agent coordinators) is consolidating from scattered job titles into a recognizable function. When multiple companies independently converge on the same organizational need, it stops being a trend piece and starts being a career path.

## Skills in Demand

The data from mid-2026 postings analysis continues to sharpen into a clear hierarchy:

- **AI fluency is becoming table stakes.** The Dice July 2026 Jobs Report found **75% of US technology job openings** required AI fluency. SHRM's Q3 review shows AI and machine learning mentions in postings up **119% YoY**. The Bipartisan Policy Center's Lightcast analysis found AI-skill postings more than doubled while overall postings stayed flat — and then jumped another **27%** through August.
- **Depth over breadth.** Revelio Labs finds that the average skill count per tech posting fell **25%** (from 30 to 21 skills), while years-of-experience requirements rose. Employers are hiring for mastery of fewer things rather than familiarity with many.
- **Agentic frameworks specifically:** experience with multi-agent orchestration, tool-use patterns, and agent evaluation is the sharpest growth area within the AI skill cluster. Postings mentioning agentic AI skills surged **587% YoY** per one dataset.
- **Human skills are not decorative.** CodeSignal's analysis of 50 million postings found communication/interpersonal skills demanded in **16%** of all postings and problem-solving in **14%**. Multiple sources list adaptability, leadership, and creative thinking alongside the technical asks. The pattern: AI handles execution, humans handle judgment and coordination.
- **Skills-based hiring is spreading.** LinkedIn reports nearly **45%** of job postings now prioritize skills over degrees. The IMF finds that postings requiring a new skill pay about **3% more** in the US and UK.
- **Cybersecurity postings are falling.** CyberSN recorded a **26.4% decline** in US cybersecurity postings — notable because security was previously treated as recession-proof. The likely driver: AI tooling is absorbing some of the monitoring and triage work that junior security analysts used to do.

**The practical signal:** the fastest route to the AI wage premium remains hands-on building, not credentials. But "AI skills" is no longer specific enough — the market is now distinguishing between someone who can prompt an LLM and someone who can architect an agent pipeline. Specificity wins.

## Policy & Institutional Moves

- **New Congress, new oversight.** After Democrats won both chambers in the November midterms, federal employee advocates are eyeing fresh opportunities to challenge the administration's workforce overhaul — including the reclassification of roughly **8,000 positions** under the Schedule Policy/Career framework and overall workforce cuts. This is the first real congressional check on federal restructuring since it began.
- **OPM finalizes personnel rules.** The Office of Personnel Management published final regulations on agency personnel management and strategic human capital management (November 10) and on managing senior professional performance (November 9). These codify changes that were previously guidance-level.
- **Federal Employee Viewpoint Survey ended.** OPM formalized the discontinuation of the governmentwide FEVS. Good-government groups warn this removes a key feedback mechanism for agency workforce health — particularly relevant as agencies try to rebuild capacity after deep cuts.
- **2027 federal pay raise in play.** With the current funding law expiring December 11, advocates are pushing for a **3–4.1% federal pay raise** in the spending deal. The outcome will signal whether Congress treats federal compensation as a retention tool or a budget variable.
- **Law enforcement pay review.** OPM issued a request for information on special pay rates for certain law enforcement personnel, aimed at January 2027 adjustments. Responses due November 25.
- **Worker classification rule advancing.** OIRA completed its review of a DOL final rule on independent contractor status. The expected rule simplifies classification around two factors: control over the work and entrepreneurial opportunity. This matters for the growing population of AI freelancers and gig workers.
- **Canada expands temporary worker access.** Effective November 6, the Low-Wage Stream of the Temporary Foreign Worker Program raised the cap from 10% to 15% of an employer's workforce and added early childhood educators to the exceptions list.

## Key Takeaway for the WFH Audience

**The restructuring has gone cross-industry, but the on-ramps are getting more specific — and that specificity is your advantage.**

The week's clearest signal is that AI-driven displacement is no longer a tech-sector story. Fiserv, Schaeffler, and Ecco represent fintech, automotive, and consumer goods — three sectors that would not have appeared in an AI layoff roundup a year ago. Meanwhile, WARN Act filings project nearly 29,000 scheduled cuts through early December. The perimeter keeps widening.

But the opportunity side is sharpening too. The market has moved past generic "AI skills" into specific, nameable functions: agentic engineering, agent operations, AI compliance, AI FinOps. Postings requiring agentic AI skills are up 587% year-over-year. And the shift to skills-based hiring (45% of postings now prioritize skills over degrees) means the barrier to entry is what you can demonstrate, not where you studied.

**If you're in transition:** pick one specialization from the emerging-roles list — agent operations and AI compliance have the widest entry points for non-ML-engineers — and build a small, demonstrable project. The market rewards specificity now. "I know AI" is background noise; "I built an agent monitoring dashboard" is a conversation starter.

**If you're watching the policy landscape:** the new Congress changes the federal workforce calculus significantly. If you're a displaced federal worker or considering federal employment, the December 11 funding deadline and the pay-raise negotiation are the near-term events to track. The worker classification rule is also worth watching if you're doing AI freelance or contract work — clearer classification rules could affect your benefits and tax treatment.

The displacement curve hasn't flattened. But the replacement roles are no longer hypothetical — they're hiring, with specific titles and specific skills. Close the gap between where you are and one of those titles. That's the move.
